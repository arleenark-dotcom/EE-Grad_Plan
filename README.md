## Purpose 
Choices you make as an undergraduate student impact your opportunities and outcomes as an electrical engineer after graduation. Your required coursework teaches you the fundamentals for your field, but much of the experience companies want from new hires can only come from building your skills outside of courses. The purpose of this plan is to give you the best practices to increase your technical and professional toolset before graduation. Figure 1, at the end, shows when each best practice fits into a four-year graduation plan.

## Planning Courses and Electives

### Choosing Major Electives
At UW Bothell, electives for EE get you started in different disciplines and fields within electrical engineering. Juan Martinez, an antenna engineering manager at Cisco, explained that companies like his hire for a specific role, so you have to be "very laser focused" on your field[^interview]. Electives give you that focus early, and knowledge in a specific EE field sticks out to employers because it shows that you have passion for engineering.

Research a class's topics before you sign up to know what the elective course covers. Email UW professors to ask when certain electives are taught, and check your picks with your academic advisor before registering so they fit your degree plan.

### Learning Electrical Engineering Tools 
Many job descriptions are specific about what tools they want EE majors to already have on hand, and a lot of these tools are taught in your classes.

Keep track of the software, equipment, and languages you use in your courses so you can add those skills to your resume or talk about them in an interview. Continue building on these tools by going beyond the required coursework, like in a personal project, so you can confidently apply those skills later on.

## Gaining Hands-On Experience

### Joining Undergraduate Research at UWB
A lot of jobs want hands-on experience, and they want you to be skilled with working in professional teams. Undergraduate research earns you professional experience in both soft skills and technical skills, working with UWB staff in project-based environments[^research]. Research also gives you mentors who can write recommendation letters for you later on.

Reach out to professors whose work lines up with your interests to learn more about their research. Most undergraduate research opportunities have a formal interview or application process, so learn about the project before you apply.

### Working on Personal Projects 
Projects look good on a resume because they show extracurricular experience, and if they're documented well, they show interest and enthusiasm for the work and highlight specific technical skills. Personal projects also earn you hands-on experience on your own time, without having to worry about deadlines.

Pick a small project that builds a skill you want, and document it with photos and a short write-up. To find project ideas, research online and ask your peers and network.

### Finding Internships
Full-time jobs prioritize applicants who have prior industry experience. Most of the time, this ends up being internship experience, because internships are specifically for students who are still learning and growing as upcoming engineers, and they let you work in industry. 

Start searching early on LinkedIn and Husky Handshake. Use UWB Career Services to find out the application seasons for internships. 

## Strategizing for Success 

### Tailoring Your Resume
Electrical engineering is a competitive job field, so your resume has to sell you as an applicant as best as possible. Companies are intentional with the keywords they use in their job postings, so tailoring your resume to include those keywords, for skills you actually have, helps you stand out.

Tailor your resume to each job you apply to. Put your most relevant projects first, and use numbers to show results where you can.

### Networking with Industry Professionals
Networking puts you out there, and it can get you far in the industry when you're looking for jobs. It also gives you more insight into the industry before you are in it, since each conversation with someone in the field gives you a new perspective and new contacts[^careers].

Use resources available to UW Bothell students to reach out to alumni and upperclassmen from UWB clubs on LinkedIn and Handshake. To create a good connection, always send a note with your initial outreach.
